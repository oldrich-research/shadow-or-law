## The programme audited this list itself, and the audit constrains how it may be quoted

Before quoting any of this, read the constraint the investigators put on their own ledger on 2026-04-28, in a
document whose entire purpose was to ask whether their closure language was overstated
(`raw_findings/2026-04-28_paradigm_circularity_audit_mechanism_no_go_ledger.md`). Its executive verdict,
verbatim:

> The no-go ledger is scientifically valid only when read as a scoped mechanism ledger. It is not valid as a
> global statement that no EM/vacuum/electron/nonstandard route to gravity can exist.

The same audit fixes the vocabulary, and the distinction it draws is the one this index would otherwise blur:

| class | meaning |
|---|---|
| `EMPIRICAL_RULED_OUT` | conflicts with measurement once the route's own physical claim is granted |
| `FRAMEWORK_INCOMPATIBLE` | the surveyed framework cannot produce the requested structure — not a theorem against all future frameworks |
| `SCOPE_CLOSED` | closed by its own mathematics, topology or declared premise; reopening requires changing the premise |
| `STILL_LIVE_IF_PREMISE_CHANGES` | not live on this route, but a materially different premise could open a new one |

So: **this is a list of routes closed inside a surveyed family, not a proof that the destination is
unreachable.** Most entries are the middle two classes. A reader looking for a way through should treat the
index as a map of where the effort has already gone, and read the fourth class as an invitation.

The audit also states what did not change as a result: the numerical relation remains an anomaly and scaffold
relation rather than a first-principles derivation, and the standard-mechanism search remains exhausted inside
the families surveyed. Both halves are reproduced because quoting either alone misrepresents it.

## How to read the verdict column

The verdict strings are the investigators' own status lines, in their own shorthand. `NO MECHANISM PASS`
means the route was reached, examined and found not to supply the required mechanism — not that it was
dismissed. Where a verdict names what the route *does* supply, that is usually the more informative half.

**Fifteen rows carry no verdict at all, and two are not routes.** Those fifteen investigations are filed in
the no-go tree — that is how they come to be in this list — but their own document states no closure line, so
the cell is left empty rather than filled with a summary invented for it. Rows 1 and 2 are the circularity
audit quoted above, which lives in the same directory. So the count of *examined routes carrying a recorded
closure* is 163, not 180. The 180 is the size of the tree, and it is the figure used elsewhere in this
document because it is the one a reader can check by listing the directory.
