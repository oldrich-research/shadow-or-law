### L01
- **object:** the one-sided Weyl denominator of SU(7) (type A₆), taken along exp(xH): D_H(x) = ∏_{α∈Φ⁺}(1−e^{−xα(H)}), with H regular. This is the best account I found. It accounts for n only.
- **n:** derived. D_H vanishes at x=0 to order |Φ⁺(A₆)| = 21, which is also dim_ℂ SU(7)/T.
- **k:** not derived. Here k = ρ(H). The value −4 needs a tuned H (chosen to fit). The canonical H = ρ^∨ gives k = 28.
- **c:** not derived. Here c = ∏α(H). The value 3 needs a tuned H (chosen to fit). The canonical value is 1!2!⋯6! = 24 883 200.
- **derivation:** D_H = e^{−xρ(H)}∏2sinh(xα(H)/2) = (∏α(H))·x²¹·e^{−xρ(H)}·(1+O(x²)). Rescaling x leaves c/|k|²¹ unchanged. For this row it is 6.8·10⁻¹³. The canonical H gives 1.0·10⁻²³, and AM–GM caps every dominant H at (2/21)²¹ ≈ 3.6·10⁻²². Since k<0, H cannot be dominant. Since c>0, H cannot be antidominant either (that would make c<0). So H must be tuned inside an intermediate chamber wC with det w = +1. The heat kernel of SU(3)×SU(3) with the basic metric, K_t(e) ≈ (4πt)⁻⁸e^{+4t}, matches k. But every heat kernel has n = −d/2 < 0. **Verdict:** n is explained; (c,k) is not.

### Recognition
**None.** No row matches a formula I know from the literature for a specific quantity.

- **The rows look like a synthetic grid.** All forty share n = 21. Every c is a ratio p/q with p, q ≤ 5, and every k is in ±{1/2, …, 5}. Rows with the same k differ only in c: five have k = 3/2, five have k = −3, and four each have k = 1/2, −2 and 3.
- **Heat kernels are the closest familiar shape, but the power has the wrong sign.**
  - The heat kernel at a point is K_t = (4πt)^{−d/2}e^{±|ρ|²t}. Compact groups give e^{+|ρ|²t} = e^{Rt/6} (Eskin, Urakawa, Fegan); complex-type G_C/G give e^{−|ρ|²t} (Gangolli); H³ gives e^{−t}.
  - Many row slopes equal a standard |ρ|²: H³ for L06 and L33; SU(2) for L07, L08 and L24; SU(3) for L20, L22, L25 and L37; Sp(2) for L03 and L09; SU(4) for L15; and the noncompact duals for positive k.
  - But n = −d/2 is always negative, so none of the rows is a heat kernel.
- **The positive-power version of the heat kernel matches no row.** That version is the Duflo–Gaussian identity ∫_𝔤 j^{1/2}(X)e^{−|X|²/4x}dX = (4πx)^{d/2}e^{−|ρ|²x}, with e^{+|ρ|²x} on the noncompact dual. Getting n = 21 needs dim 𝔤 = 42. That forces c = (4π)²¹, and |k| = 7/4 with the Killing form or |k| ≥ 7 with the basic form. No row has these values.
- **Natural products fail by orders of magnitude.**
  - Weyl denominators in their natural chamber, Kostant-type q-products and K-theoretic Euler classes all satisfy c/|k|²¹ ≤ (2/21)²¹ ≈ 3.6·10⁻²².
  - Poisson laws give exactly 1/21! ≈ 2·10⁻²⁰, and Gamma laws give k/21!.
  - The rows range from 2.6·10⁻¹⁵ (L15) to 3.5·10⁶ (L35), so even the closest is about seven orders of magnitude too large.
- **Fitting carries no evidential weight.** Any c ∈ ℚ₍>0₎ and k ∈ ½ℤ can be reproduced by some q-product or reciprocal Hilbert series, as the alternatives in L03, L09, L16, L24, L30, L33, L37 and L39 show.
- **One more near-miss is the circular hydrogenic state.** For n = 22, l = 21 it is R ∝ r²¹e^{−Zr/22}, which has exactly this form. But its normalized c is (2k)^{22.5}/√(44!), about 10⁻⁵ even at k = 5, and k can never be negative. So it is not any of these rows either.
